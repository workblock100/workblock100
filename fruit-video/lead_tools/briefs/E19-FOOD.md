## Part 1: the kit
1. Within 20 minutes: `GUIDE_FOOD19.md` from the contract and the first batch (`steam`, `roll`, `rolls`, `basket`, `plate` with `sticker`, `coffee`). Then batch 2 (the turkey family, `turkeyMold`, `pie`, `pieMold`, `blender`, `pour`, `viscosity`), then batch 3. After each batch run `node --check ep19_food.js`, your harness, and look at a contact sheet of the batch (every dish at three times on a neutral background AND on the wall/counter colours of the series).
2. Quality bar (this is the series' first food; it must look appetising where it should and gloriously beige where it should not): a roast turkey with a glossy golden skin, a darker crust at the legs, a few specular highlights, a ring of garnish; pecan pie with a ring of glazed pecans and a crimped crust; rolls with a soft sheen and a flour dusting under a gingham cloth; the beige molds are smooth, matte-shiny and perfectly shaped with a little piped detail, in a creamy beige that is deliberately drab next to the golden turkey; coffee steam that curls and thins; a slow cooker with a glass lid that fogs, a lid that rattles on its own steam (a small deterministic bounce) and a small red KEEP WARM light; jello with a believable wobble; the can of jellied cranberry sauce that holds the can's ridges when it slides out.
3. Viscosity is the showpiece: `F19.pour` has to make a viewer feel water versus honey versus pudding in 20 frames; honey folds on itself in a slow rope; pudding is a lump that hangs, stretches, drops and wobbles. Draw it as a smooth tapered ribbon (a bezier stream with a changing width and a slow lateral wave), not as particles.
4. Determinism and cost as in the contract (cached sprites, pure functions). The test: same call twice identical; frame t drawn first or last identical.

Study the model cards first: `ep18_title.js` (an exit sign and a pull station; an end card that is the drill log under a chirping detector, with a teaser), `ep17_title.js`, `ep16_title.js`. Cue times: `python3 /tmp/shift_tools/cues.py 19 title` and `... end`. `window.FP19A` exports like `FP18A` if the family does it; a constant `TEASER20 = 'THE SNOW DAY'` at the top of the file.
- **title (6.8 s)**: a close, warm kitchen-counter frame, evening: a slow cooker centred with its glass lid, the dial on KEEP WARM and a small red light, a gingham cloth on the counter, a wooden spoon, a recipe card taped beside it that reads EPISODE 19 / THE POTLUCK (UI label). The lid rattles (`lidrattle` 1.0 and 3.0 s: the lid hops with a small bounce, steam puffs), steam rises from the lid (`steam` 1.6 s) and curls into the letters SHIFT HAPPENS (the steam condenses on the glass of a window behind it, or simply forms the title in the air, readable and lit warmly; the letters must pass the text gate: draw the title through the UI kit and let steam move around and across it at low alpha, never covering more than a light haze), the title lands warm gold at about 2.8 s; `textding` 5.2 s lights the recipe card. Nothing static: a slow push-in of 2 percent, the dial's light pulses, dust motes in the lamp light.
- **end (7.5 s)**: the sign-up sheet as the end card (a GridSheet-style card or a clipboard sheet through the UI kit): rows ROLLS: 1 (ELEVEN SIGNED UP), TURKEY: BEIGE, PIE: SMOOTHIE, COFFEE: PUDDING, BREAK: 0:00, TEMPERATURE LOG: 78 F; a HAPPY THANKSGIVING stamp lands at `stamp` (3.9 s); small cast chips as in `ep18_title.js`; `typing` 1.0 s as rows type in; a teaser line NEXT: EPISODE 20 / THE SNOW DAY (`textding` 5.0 s). A single small beige turkey mold sits in the corner as a mascot. Hold the last frame calm.
Gate both with `bash /tmp/shift_tools/audit_scene.sh 19 title` and `... end` (`0 issue-frames`).
