## THE ALIGNMENT CONTRACT (the reason this episode exists)
The user has watched earlier episodes and said "a lot of shit is off": upside-down sheets, names printed over each other, posters sliced by the frame edge, banners running off the side, labels not centred in their pills, text off the ruled lines of a page. Our old audit never looked at text. It does now (`txaudit.js`, the "text gate"; it runs inside every audit of this episode). Your scenes must pass it AND look right to a human at full resolution.

1. **All text goes through the UI kit** (`ui.js`, `window.UI`; the API is in `/tmp/shift_tools/briefs/UIKIT.md` and, when it is finished, `GUIDE_UI.md` in the project folder). `UI.label` centres on the ink and the cap band; `UI.sign` / `UI.pill` / `UI.panel` register their container so centring and containment are verified; `UI.page` / `UI.onRule` put text ON ruled lines; `UI.table` gives cells that cannot overlap; `UI.stack` centres title cards. No raw `ctx.fillText` unless a brief says so. No text drawn into offscreen/cached canvases (the gate cannot see it; draw text live every frame). If the kit is not ready when you need it, build everything else first (check `grep -c "UI.label" ui.js`), and never write your own text layout code around it.
2. **Signs, posters, banners, boards and sheets are fully in the frame (hero text keeps 40 px from the edge) or entirely out of it. Never sliced.** If a camera move would slice one, move the sign, change the move, or hide it for those shots. A whip pan may carry text through the edge for a few frames (a fast pass is exempt). Nothing under the caption zone (y 1380 to 1700) or the clock pill (x 48 to 500, y 116 to 244) except in scenes that turn those off (`noScrim`, `noChyron`).
3. **Text is upright and unmirrored.** At most about 12 degrees of tilt (stickers, stamps; pass `tilt` for more, sparingly). Never draw text with a flipped or sheared transform. For text on a surface in perspective (a sign on a hall wall), place it at the projected centre with a uniform scale (see how the room-number plates do it); never mirror it.
4. **No text over text.** Two different strings never overlap (stacked notes, a signature over a printed name, a sound word on a label). Lay tables out in columns.
5. **Hero text** (anything the viewer must read: signs, inserts, HUD, name cards, stamps) is drawn with `hero: true` (the UI kit does this by default): at least 30 px on screen, never covered, never under a caption or the clock pill. Set dressing is `hero: false`.
6. **Characters**: every face fully inside the frame (already audited), and no more than a sliver of a body cut by an edge unless it is walking in or out (`transit: true`). NO blurred foreground characters and no over-the-shoulder blur: a character in a shot is sharp and complete, or out of the shot. Background figures are small and keep clear of faces and text. No character stands in front of a hero sign.
7. **Things in a row line up**: feet on one floor line, equal gaps (`UI.rowX`), centred things on the same axis (measure with the gate or a pixel check, do not eyeball), symmetric layouts truly symmetric. Snap layout numbers to a grid (`UI.snap`, 8 px).
8. **Look at it.** The gate cannot judge taste. After every audit pass, render stills at full size (every 0.5 s through your scenes, plus every moment a text element first appears) and look at them with the Read tool (view at least 540 px wide; crop with PIL for detail). `python3 /tmp/shift_tools/atlas.py 13 <scene> 0.25` tiles a full-resolution crop of EVERY string in a scene (time, size, angle): read every sheet, letter by letter, and fix anything that is off even when the gate is green. Your report must say what you looked at and what you changed because of it.

Run the gate while you work: `bash /tmp/shift_tools/audit_scene.sh 13 <scene> 0.1` (3x cheaper) and, for your final pass, the default (every frame): it must print `0 issue-frames` (NOTE lines do not fail; read them and fix the ones you can). A hero-text note is an error, a dressing note is a to-do.
