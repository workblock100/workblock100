## The scene (10:59 PM to 7:00 AM, scene-local seconds)
Setting: the nurses' station at night on the generator: half the ceiling lights are off, a few red-amber emergency lamps and the green exit sign glow, a desk lamp, the wall clock reading 10:59; a big window behind the station with the blizzard, a deep snow sill (`SN20.sill` with `depth` from `SN20.depthAt` of the world clock); the med cart with a 3-11 sticker on its side; cots against the back wall with Mango, Lemon, Tomato and Onion asleep under grey blankets (`P20.cot`). Strawberry alone at the cart. The TV is dark. The coffee maker is dead. Use `L20.pass` for the night look (ambient about .35, cool window rect, warm desk lamp, red-amber emergency points); every face lit (L2).
- `open` 0.20-1.80 (`clocktick` 0.40): a slow push-in on the empty station, the snow in the window, the sleeping staff, Strawberry standing at the cart looking at the glass doors (an establishing shot, 2 s at most).
- `s1` 1.90 Strawberry "Is the oncoming nurse here?" (she looks at the door, then at the clock; breath fogs her words in the cold building) | `wait` 3.32-4.82 (`windgust` 3.52; ticks): the door, the clock ticking to 11:00 (UI kit digits, hero when at rest), snow hissing on the glass, nobody comes; her shoulders drop.
- `s2` 4.87 "I am the oncoming nurse." (a slow turn to the camera). THE TWIN SHOT from 6.25 to 10.40: ONE character drawn TWICE in one frame, facing each other, a two-shot at table height: on the left the OUTGOING Strawberry (a 3-11 badge, slumped), on the right the ONCOMING Strawberry (she has peeled her badge sticker and re-stuck it as 11-7: `badgepeel` 6.77), mirrored by an `x` flip of the whole figure (or `yaw`). Only the character who is speaking is bound to the voice (`who: 'straw'`: the mouth moves); the other copy is silent (no `who`). Use two slightly different `look` values and a different blink phase so it reads as two. | `s3` 6.25 (outgoing) "Report. Everyone is alive." | `s4` 7.94 (oncoming, with a tablet and a pen) "Any concerns?" | `s5` 8.97 (outgoing) "I am tired." | `s6` 9.85 (oncoming, writing it down) "Noted." The shots change at least every 3 s and between the speakers (over-the-shoulder on each, then the two-shot).
- `lapse` 10.65-18.65 (8.0 s; `lapsewhoosh` 10.65, `clockspin` 10.85, `windswell` 11.65): THE TIME-LAPSE, the visual showpiece of the scene: ONE locked-off wide of the station and its window, the world clock running from 11:00 PM (1380 minutes) to 7:00 AM (1860) while the video runs 8 s. Speed curve (minutes of world time per video second): ease in over 0.7 s from 1x, hold about 66 min/s, ease out over the last 0.9 s so the shot lands on 7:00 AM at (nearly) real time and the Moon's line is in real time. In `ep20_lapse_sim.js`: `worldMin(lt)` (the piecewise-eased integral, exactly 1380 at 10.65 and 1860 at 18.65), `speed(lt)` (min per second), `sky(lt)` (night blue to predawn violet to a gold sunrise), `moon(lt)` (his arc across the window pane: high at 11 PM, setting at about 6:50, large and low at 7:00 for his line), `depth(lt)` (`SN20.depthAt(worldMin)`), `staff(lt)` (who is where). What the viewer sees: the wall clock's hands whirl; the window sky changes colour through the whole range and the stars/Moon cross it; the snow rises on the sill and drifts up the glass; Strawberry does her rounds again and again as a ghost trail (the figure repeated 5 or 6 times at fading alpha at earlier positions of its path, motion trails instead of blur: L3), the call lights over two doors flash red slowly (at most 1 Hz, dim), the cot sleepers change pose in jumps and one sits up and lies down; the desk lamp flickers once. Everything else is still: that stillness is what makes it read. DO NOT put text on the screen except the wall clock and, if you like, a small UI-kit chip with the world time in the corner (non-hero, no snow over it). `tools_lapse.js` writes `build/ep20/lapse.json`: `[{ "t": <ABSOLUTE seconds>, "worldMin": ..., "speed": <min per second>, "sun": 0..1, "moonX": ... }]` sampled at 10 Hz over the whole scene (outside the lapse speed is 1/60 min per s, the real-time rate), so the sound can speed the room tone, the wind and the ticks with the picture.
- `mo1` 18.70 the Moon, low and large in the window pane, setting, gentle, glowing: "Good morning, three to eleven." (golden light begins to fall across the room) | `s7` 20.84 Strawberry "What time is it?" (she looks up at the clock: 7:00) | `mo2` 21.85 "Seven." | `s8` 22.90 "In the morning?" | `mo3` 23.72 "Yes." | `s9` 24.78 "Okay." (flat, a slow blink; a tiny, tired smile is allowed).
- `dawn` 25.69-27.69 (`plowdist` 25.99): the snow has stopped; golden sunrise floods the room; the world outside is white and silent; the cots' sleepers stir; a long calm shot with a slow push-in; the plow's amber beacon appears at the window edge. | `tu3` 27.74 Turnip's plow passes close in the window, he raises his mug: "I plowed the lot." (`A20.truck` / `A20.turnip` in the pane; a wave of snow slides up the glass doors in the background: the door is buried again) | `o5` 29.61 Onion (sitting up on her cot in her blanket, snow melting on her shoulders from last night? no: just rumpled, crying) "The entrance is blocked." | `s10` 31.34 Strawberry "Of course." | `end` 32.41-33.81 (sting 32.51): a held beat: Strawberry small at the med cart in warm sunrise light, the buried door behind her, the sleeping cots, the snow glittering.
Shots at least every 3 s (the lapse is ONE shot by design: keep it moving); every line has a reaction; speakers 300 px or taller while speaking, except the two-shot (each Strawberry at least 260 px).
