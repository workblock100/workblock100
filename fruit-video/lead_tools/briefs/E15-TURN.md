## The problem
Every character in Episodes 6 to 14 is a front-facing sprite (`V2C.drawChar` in `v2_char.js`, helpers in `engine.js`: `bodyPath`, `drawTexture`, `drawTop`, `shoulder`, `poseAngles`, `drawAcc`, `KINDS`, `EXT.acc`). They can look left and right with their eyes, and walk sideways with a gait, but never turn: there is no three-quarter view, no profile, no back. That is the biggest remaining limit of the whole series. Episode 15 needs: an audience seen from behind watching a TV, conversations in three-quarter view, staff walking in profile along a corridor (a zipper merge), characters walking away.

## What to build: `o.yaw` (radians)
`drawChar({..., yaw})`: yaw 0 = front (EXACTLY today's output, see the regression rule), positive turns the character's FACE toward screen right, negative toward the left, plus or minus pi/2 is profile, plus or minus pi is the back (wrap: yaw is taken modulo 2 pi). Treat each fruit as a spheroid rotating about its vertical axis (a "sphere-mapped" rig, the way 2D animators turn round characters):
1. **Face**: map every facial feature onto the head ellipsoid. Eyes sit at longitudes plus and minus `asin(eyeX/R)`, the mouth, nose-like marks and cheeks at their own longitudes; after the yaw each feature's x is `R sin(lon + yaw)` and its horizontal scale is `cos(lon + yaw)` (clamped so a feature near the limb is a thin sliver, never negative), hidden beyond the horizon (cos < 0.05). The pupils keep following `look`; lids, lashes, brows (follow their eye, tilt with the perspective), cheeks, bags, sparkle: all follow. The mouth keeps lip-sync (`o.mouthState`, open/spread/teeth) in three-quarter and profile (a profile mouth is a curved opening at the silhouette edge, with a bit of the lip overhanging the edge so the profile reads). At profile the face must still read as a face: one big eye near the edge, brow, mouth. At the back there is no face.
2. **Body**: the silhouette of a spheroid does not change with yaw, so `bodyPath` stays (kinds that are not rotationally symmetric are special: `banana` (curved), `star`, `pill`/capsule, `cherry` pair, `grape` bunch etc.: take them one at a time; a reasonable rule is that a non-symmetric kind mirrors its silhouette for yaw beyond plus or minus pi/2 and squeezes toward the profile; document what you chose). Surface texture (`drawTexture`: strawberry seeds, citrus dimples, melon netting, mango blush, etc.) and stems/leaves/crowns (`drawTop`) must rotate with the body (seeds map onto the sphere too: x = R sin(lon + yaw), flattened by cos; leaves and stems are drawn on the correct side). The lighting (`shade`) stays in screen space (the key light does not rotate).
3. **Arms**: shoulders sit at plus and minus 90 degrees of longitude, so after the yaw their x is `shoulderX cos(yaw)`; the near arm is drawn after the body, the far arm before it (hidden behind the body at profile). Poses (`poseAngles`) are defined in the frontal plane: for yaw they foreshorten (x scale cos); add a forward/back swing axis for walking and gestures (a `swingZ` per arm) so that in profile the arms swing along the walking direction. `grip` targets (IK) stay in screen space. Hands: fine as drawn.
4. **Legs and shoes**: hips at plus and minus `hip` along the frontal axis foreshorten by cos(yaw); in profile the two legs overlap and the gait swings them along the walking direction (the existing distance-driven gait already moves feet along x: keep planted feet exact; no sliding). The far leg is drawn first (a touch darker). Shoes orient toward the facing direction (toe toward the face side) and the shoe's visible length grows with sin(yaw). Back view: heels toward the camera.
5. **Accessories** (`drawAcc`, `EXT.acc.<name>`: nurse cap, stethoscope, badges, lanyards, glasses, ties, hair bows, mustaches, hats, etc.): each existing accessory used by the main cast must be yaw-aware: front-of-face items (glasses, mustache, lashes, name badge on the chest) hide or foreshorten with the face; things on top (cap, hat) rotate with a simple perspective (cap crosses visible only from the front and three-quarter); things around the neck show a back side; the ID badge on a lanyard flips to its blank back. Provide a generic fallback (foreshorten by cos and slide by sin on the face side) and a per-accessory override table `YAW_ACC[name] = (o, k, t, layer, yaw) => ...` for the important ones: `cap`, `stethoscope`, `badge`/`idbadge`/`lanyard`, `glasses`, `tie`, `suit`/`collar`, `scrubs` if they exist. Search `EXT.acc.` in the project to list all accessories (earlier art kits `ep7_art.js`, `ep9_art.js`, `ep10_art.js`, `ep11_art.js`, `ep13_art.js`, `ep14_art.js` register more).
6. **Held props and the registry**: the layout/text gate registers boxes (`regLocal('face'|'head'|'body'|'prop', ...)`). A face that is not visible at the back must not register as a face box that fails audits; boxes follow the visible geometry.
7. **Shadows, reflections, silhouette functions** (`floorShadow`, `reflection`, `legs(.., silhouette)`) must respect yaw too (a profile body is the same width, the feet differ).
8. **API sugar**: `o.yaw`, plus helper `V2C.faceDir(o)` (returns +1/-1 for which way the face points on screen) and `yawToward(dx)` (a smooth mapping from a velocity or a target x to a yaw), documented in `GUIDE_TURN.md` in the project folder (create it in the worktree; short: the contract, the helpers, the kinds that are special, a code snippet for a profile walker, a seated audience member seen from behind, a three-quarter talker). Also `o.turnTo = [yaw0, yaw1, u]` or a small helper to ease a turn over time (a head turn: let the HEAD lead the body: `o.headYaw` (face features and top) distinct from `o.yaw` (body, arms, legs) so a character can turn only the head, which is the most useful animation in a conversation).
