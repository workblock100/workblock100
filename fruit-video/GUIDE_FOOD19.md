## Rules for every function
- `F19.dish(ctx, kind, o)` and `F19.steam(ctx, o)` draw into the context you pass (`ctx`), in WORLD units, in the transform in force (the scene camera). Pure functions of their options and `t`: no `Math.random`, no clock, no state: frame N is the same in every worker and every run (tested). Two instances of the same dish want different `seed`s (it varies the roll shapes, the cloth angle, the ripple phase).
- Each call saves and restores the context. The static body of a dish is baked once into an offscreen canvas (keyed by kind, view, style and the pixel density the current transform needs) and blitted; only steam, liquids, lids, wobble and plate items are live. A dish costs about 0.3 to 1.5 ms warm (numbers at the end).
- No text is ever baked. Stickers, labels and the dial legend are drawn live through the UI kit and are set dressing (`hero: false, crop: true`) unless you pass `hero: true` (a close-up at rest: then the gate holds them to 30 px, the 40 px margin and "not covered"). Never put a hand, a character or steam over a hero sticker.
- Nothing is drawn at `s < .02` or `alpha <= 0` (the first frame of an `eb()` pop): the call still returns its record, so layout code never needs a branch.
- Units: local y runs DOWN the screen, so a dish rises to negative y. `(x, y)` = the bottom centre of the footprint (view `'top'`: the centre). Native size at `s = 1` (side view): rolls, basket, plate, pie, casserole about 300 px wide; one roll 160; a paper cup 120; a mug 190 with its handle. A standing adult is about 400 px tall at `s = 1`, so a plate at `s = .7` fits a hand.
- `F19.dish` returns `{ w, h, top: [x, y], hot: [x, y] }`: the drawn size and two anchors in WORLD coordinates (rotation and scale included): `top` the top centre (hang a sticker, a hand, a lid), `hot` where steam rises from. `F19.info(kind, o)` returns the same record without drawing.
- Common options: `x, y, s, rot, t, seed, steam (0..1), shadow (default true), alpha (0..1), view ('side' | 'top')`.

## Dishes (batch 1)
- **`roll`**: one dinner roll, golden dome, flour dust, a slash on top, a soft sheen. Side or top. `F19.dish(ctx, 'roll', { x: 540, y: 1000, s: 1.2, seed: 3 })`.
- **`basket`**: the empty wicker basket lined with a red and white gingham cloth whose two corners hang over the front rim (side), or the woven ring on a gingham square set on the diagonal (top; the cloth corners stick out). `F19.dish(ctx, 'basket', { x, y, s: .8 })`.
- **`rolls`**: a basket of rolls under the checked cloth. `n` 1..12 rolls showing (they fill the pile in a fixed order: the middle first), `cover` 0..1 (side view default .5: a napkin corner folded over the back left of the pile, hiding the slots behind it; top view default 0: a gingham flap over the edge of the rolls; 0 removes it), `seed` (shapes, cloth angle in the top view). Top view: the cloth is a 270 px square turned by a seed angle, so its corners poke out of the 266 px ring: eleven baskets on a table want about 330 px of room each at `s = 1`. `F19.dish(ctx, 'rolls', { x, y, s: .55, n: 11, seed: i, view: 'top' })`. Steam: a faint warm haze (`steam: 1`).
- **`plate`**: a paper plate with a fluted rim. `items`: kind strings placed by the kit in slots (1 to 6 items), each scaled to look right on the plate, back to front by depth: `['turkeyMold', 'roll', 'pieMold', 'coffee']`. An entry may be an object `{ kind, k (size multiplier), dx, dy, ...any option of that kind }` (for example `{ kind: 'coffee', thick: 1, fill: .9 }`). `itemScale` multiplies all. `stickers`: `[{ kind, text, at: [fx, fy], s, rot }]` stuck on the plate (`at` is a fraction of the plate's box: [0, 0] top left, [1, 1] bottom right; `s` defaults to .7). `steam` is passed on to the items that steam (coffee). `view: 'top'` shows the plate and its items from above. Items are drawn `bare` (see below: no platter of their own, no garnish), so a beige turkey sits straight on the paper plate; pass `bare: false` in an item object to give it its platter back. `F19.dish(ctx, 'plate', { x, y, s: 1, items: ['roll'], stickers: [{ kind: 'note', text: 'FOR STRAWBERRY', at: [.2, .2] }] })`. Items whose kind has not landed yet are skipped silently.
- **`coffee`**: a paper cup (corrugated sleeve) or, with `mug: true`, a ceramic mug with a red band. `thick` 0..1: 0 liquid (a dark coffee surface, ripples, a specular streak, steam), .35 nectar (smoother, browner), .7 honey (amber, glossier, a slow swirl, little steam), 1 pudding (a matte tan dome heaped above the rim when the cup is nearly full, no steam, no ripples). `fill` 0..1 (default .85: how full the cup is). The liquid is drawn live, so `thick` and `fill` can animate smoothly. `steam` 0..1 curls from the surface centre (`hot`) for `thick` below about .6. Top view: the cup from above. `F19.dish(ctx, 'coffee', { x, y, s: 1, thick: .35, fill: .9, steam: 1, t })`.

## Dishes (batch 2)
All native widths are about 300 to 340 px at `s = 1` (a platter is 336 wide); the plate kit scales them down for you.
- **`turkey`**: the whole roast bird on a gold-ringed platter: a golden glazed body (darker crust toward the edge, a breastbone line, skin speckle, specular glints), two wings, two drumsticks in front with bone knuckles and white paper frills, a garnish ring (parsley, cranberries, orange slices). `carve` 0..1 (slices taken from the right side, quantised to sixths, so it steps as you animate it): a cream cut face with grain appears on the right of the breast, at 4 and up a bone shows, up to four slices lie on the platter. Steams gently. `F19.dish(ctx, 'turkey', { x, y, s: .9, steam: 1, carve: 0 })`. `hot` = the top of the breast.
- **`turkeySlices`**: seven fanned slices (`n` 3..10) with a golden skin edge, parsley and berries. **`turkeyChop`**: a heap of chopped pieces (about 58 irregular cubes, with skin flecks). **`turkeyMince`**: a crumbly mound (a few hundred crumbs, a noisy outline). **`turkeyPuree`**: a smooth beige scoop with a ruffled foot. Each on a smaller platter (300 wide). The stages of the kitchen's conveyor, in order: `turkey`, `turkeySlices`, `turkeyChop`, `turkeyMince`, (the blender), `turkeyPuree`, `turkeyMold`.
- **`turkeyMold`**: the SAME silhouette as `turkey` (same platter, same size, same anchors: swap one for the other in place) in `F19.BEIGE`: matte, smooth, with a soft sheen (`shine` 0..1, default .6), a piped rope down the breastbone, four piped rosettes, a scalloped hem of beads along the base, rosettes where the frills were. Faint steam. It is meant to be funny because it is so neat and so beige next to the golden one.
- **`pie`**: a pecan pie in a ribbed foil tin: a crimped golden crust ring, a glossy dark filling, a ring of glazed pecans, an inner ring and a centre one, specular glints. `slice` 0..1 (quantised to twelfths): a wedge (up to 86 degrees) missing at the front right, showing the tin floor and the layered cut wall (glaze, filling, crust). Steams. **`pieMold`**: the same pie in beige with no tin: a fluted side, a ring of piped beads for the crust, beige pecans; `slice` works too (the wedge shows beige layers). **`pieSlice`**: one wedge on its side (cut face toward the viewer: crust, filling with pecans, glaze), 170 wide; `mold: true` makes it beige.
- **`blender`**: a glass jar on a dark base with a dial, a handle and a lid. `fill` 0..1 (the level of the contents), `color` (the contents: default beige; pass `'#8a5a2c'` for pie), `on` 0..1 (spinning: the jar shakes, the surface dips into a vortex, light and dark streaks sweep across the glass: pass `t`), `lid` 0..1 (0 seated; 1 lifted 96 px above the jar and tilted: the open mouth shows, drop things in). 190 wide, 270 tall. `hot` = the top of the jar. `F19.dish(ctx, 'blender', { x, y, s: 1, fill: .6, on: seg(lt, a, b), lid: 1 - seg(lt, c, d), t: lt })`.

## Dishes (batch 3)
- **`slowcooker`**: a chrome base with black handles and a black foot band, a dial (OFF LOW HIGH WARM detents, a white pointer at `dial`: `'OFF' | 'LOW' | 'HIGH' | 'WARM'`, default `'WARM'`), the legend KEEP WARM on the black band (live UI text: set dressing, or `hero: true` for a close-up: it is 14 local units tall, so it passes the 30 px rule from `s` about 2.2 up), a small lamp (`light` 0..1: dim red to a glowing red with an additive halo; use FX18.glow when it is loaded), a cream stoneware crock with stew that bubbles (bubbles grow, pop in a ring, always, even without steam), and a glass lid with a chrome rim, a vent notch, a black knob and condensation (`fog` 0..1, default .5, quantised to quarters). `lid` -1..1: the lid TIPS about the far rim (sign = which side rises: + the right, - the left; 1 = about 8 px of lift and .15 rad), `hop` 0..1 lifts it straight up (1 = 40 px). With `steam > 0` the lid also rattles by itself: a deterministic bounce of up to 2 px (about 8 small knocks a second, in bursts that swell and fade every second or so) and a tiny rock, so a slow cooker on KEEP WARM is never static. Steam leaves the vent (`hot`, at the right rim) and, when the lid is lifted or tipped, extra steam leaves the gap on the rising side. 330 wide, 250 tall. The title card drives `lid`/`hop` from the `lidrattle` cues and `light` from a slow sine. `F19.dish(ctx, 'slowcooker', { x: 540, y: 1100, s: 2.4, steam: .9, light: .8, hop: bounce(lt), t: lt, hero: true })`.
- **`jello`**: a fluted lime (or `color`) gelatin mold with fruit pieces suspended in it, glossy highlights and a glassy top, on a small plate. `wobble` 0..1 and `t0`: from `t0` the mold wobbles about its base (the jiggle, amplitude .16 x `wobble`, 3.4 Hz, damp 2.4) and it also breathes by a hair always. 190 wide, 150 tall. `F19.dish(ctx, 'jello', { x, y, s: 1.2, wobble: 1, t0: 12.4, t: lt })`.
- **`cranCan`**: the cylinder of jellied cranberry sauce standing on a plate, glossy burgundy with the can's ridges pressed round it (they follow the height), a highlight streak. `slices` 0..4: slices cut off the top (the cylinder gets shorter by 24 px each) lie fanned on the plate to the right. 250 wide, 150 tall at `slices: 0`.
- **`casserole`**: a glass dish with two glass lugs, green bean casserole showing through the glass, a crispy-onion top, and foil: `foil` 0..1 (quantised to quarters; default .78) = how much of the top the foil covers, from the left, with a torn free edge, a crimped skirt over the lip and crinkle lines. `foil: 0` shows the onions. 330 wide. Steams.
- **`pizzaBox`**: a cardboard box with a red stripe and a pizza-slice icon on the lid. `open` 0..1 (quantised to eighths): the lid swings up on its back hinge (105 degrees at 1) and the pizza shows: crust, sauce, cheese, pepperoni, a sheen. Steams when open.

## Liquids: `F19.viscosity(thick)` and `F19.pour(ctx, o)`
`F19.viscosity(thick)` turns the coffee's `thick` (0 liquid, .35 nectar, .7 honey, 1 pudding) into the pour's `visc` (0 water, .25 nectar, .5 honey, 1 pudding): piecewise linear through those four pairs; `F19.thickOf(visc)` is the inverse. The same curve drives the cup (`thick`) and the stream (`visc`), so the kitchen's gag and the plate's coffee pudding agree.

`F19.pour(ctx, { x0, y0, x1, y1, t, t0, t1, visc, color, width, keep })`: a stream from the spout at `(x0, y0)` to the surface at `(x1, y1)`, the head leaving the spout at `t0` and the tail at `t1`. Returns `{ lands, ends }`: the scene time the first liquid reaches `(x1, y1)` and the time the last does: call it once with `t: -9` (it draws nothing) to read them, then drive the cup's `fill` from them. It draws ON TOP of what is already there: draw the pot and the cup first. It does not draw the spout or the pot. `width` = the stream's width at the spout (default 14; the larger the closer the camera). `color` defaults to the coffee colour that matches `visc`. `keep: true` leaves the mound at the surface after the pour (default: it settles into the surface over about 1.4 s after `ends`).
- **visc 0 (water)**: a thin straight stream that accelerates (it narrows as it falls), lands in about 9 frames over 300 px, throws droplets and two ripple rings, and the tail falls away as a short streak.
- **visc .25 (nectar)**: the same but a little slower and wider, with a faint beading on the way down.
- **visc .5 (honey)**: slow (about 25 frames over 300 px), a rope that thins to a third of its width as it falls, sways in a slow lateral wave, and COILS where it lands: a low mound of overlapping folds (about 1.25 x `width` high) that grows and orbits. After `t1` the tail thins to a thread and detaches.
- **visc .7**: the rope develops beads (necking), still a stream.
- **visc 1 (pudding)** and anything from .78 up: it will not pour. A lump bulges out of the spout (.5 s), hangs and stretches on a thinning neck (.55 s), the neck snaps (a thread whips back into the spout), the lump falls as an egg, lands, squashes (wide and low for a tenth of a second) and wobbles (a damped 2.9 Hz wobble) into a dome about 1.1 x the lump's radius wide; a new lump every 1.55 s while `t0 .. t1` lasts (at least one). Lumps are about 1.5 x `width` in radius, so give it `width: 30` or more for a close shot. Later lumps stack on the earlier ones.
Example: `const r = F19.pour(ctx, { x0: 420, y0: 700, x1: 420, y1: 1010, t: lt, t0: 4.0, t1: 6.5, visc: F19.viscosity(thick), width: 22 });` with the cup drawn first at `fill` rising from `r.lands` to `r.ends`.

## Steam: `F19.steam(ctx, { x, y, w, h, t, seed, power, curl, t0, t1, period, stagger, n, lean })`
Two to five thin curling wisps rising from `(x, y)` (the middle of the source). `w` = how wide the source is and how far the wisps sway, `h` = how high they climb (default 150), `power` 0..1 (alpha and number of wisps), `curl` 0..1 (default .6, how much the tips curl). Cream white, normal blend, alpha at most about .5, soft: it reads on mid and dark grounds and is a faint haze on a pale wall. Each wisp rises over 2.3 to 4 s and fades; it never covers more than a thin ribbon, but keep it off hero text anyway (the title card lets it drift across the title at low alpha on purpose and passes the gate). About 1.2 ms for five wisps.
Added for the title card: `t0` (the plume starts at `t0`: nothing is drawn before it; each wisp is born within `stagger` x its period after `t0`, default .9), `t1` (emission stops at `t1`; wisps in flight finish), `period` (seconds for a wisp's head to climb the whole height: default 2.3 to 4; a short one makes a fast burst: `period: .65, h: 1000` shoots a jet up in about a second), `n` (the number of wisps, 1..14; default 2 to 5 from `power`), `lean` (px of sideways drift at the top, growing with height: a draught; negative leans left).
`F19.steam(ctx, { x: d.hot[0], y: d.hot[1], w: 40, h: 200, t: lt, seed: 5, power: .8 })`; a burst on a cue: `F19.steam(ctx, { x, y, w: 120, h: 1040, t: lt, seed: 5, t0: cue, t1: cue + .65, period: .65, n: 9, stagger: .5, lean: -250 })`.

## Stickers: `F19.sticker(ctx, { kind, x, y, s, rot, text, t, t0, hero })`
`(x, y)` = the centre. Kinds: `'note'` (a yellow sticky note, handwriting font Lucky; `\n` makes lines; 118 x 108 at `s = 1`), `'sticker'` (a round scalloped badge with a tiny turkey; the first word of `text` goes on line one, the rest on line two: `'FUN COMMITTEE'`; 118 across), `'label'` (a white allergen label with a red bar and a printed barcode; `'CONTAINS: BREAD'` puts `CONTAINS:` small and `BREAD` big; 150 x 70), `'tag'` (a manila hang tag with a string; 112 x 74), `'clip'` (a binder clip, no text; 64 x 76). `t0`: the moment it is stuck on: it slaps down over .2 s (scale 1.3 to 1, a small rotation settle) and nothing is drawn before `t0`. Returns the box `[x0, y0, x1, y1]` in the current transform (rotation included). `rot` over about 11 degrees is allowed (the kit passes `tilt` to the text), but stay under about 25. Text is set dressing unless `hero: true`.

## Jiggle: `F19.jiggle(t, t0, amp = .12, freq = 3.2, damp = 3)`
Returns `{ sx, sy, skew }`: identity before `t0`, then a damped wobble (`sy = 1 + a`, `sx = 1 - a / 2`, `skew` leans with the quarter-period phase; `a = amp e^(-damp tau) sin(2 pi freq tau)`). Multiply it onto anything: `const j = F19.jiggle(lt, t0); ctx.translate(x, y); ctx.transform(j.sx, 0, j.skew, j.sy, 0, 0);`.

## Top views and `bare`
`view: 'top'` (origin = the centre of the dish) exists for every kind: `roll`, `rolls`, `basket`, `plate`, `coffee` (a cup, or with `mug: true` a mug with its handle), `turkey`, `turkeyMold`, `turkeySlices`, `turkeyChop`, `turkeyMince`, `turkeyPuree`, `pie`, `pieMold` (a wedge cut with `slice`), `jello`, `cranCan` (slices lie beside it), `casserole`, `slowcooker`, `pizzaBox`, `blender` (the lid and handle only). `pieSlice` has none (it falls back to its side view). A plate with `view: 'top'` draws its items from above. Sizes in top view are about the same as in side view (a platter or plate about 300 to 370 px across).
`bare: true` (turkey family: `turkey`, `turkeyMold`, `turkeySlices`, `turkeyChop`, `turkeyMince`, `turkeyPuree`): the dish without its own platter and without its garnish, lowered so that its footprint is on y = 0: for a dish that sits on a plate of its own. `plate` passes it for you.

## Cards: `ep19_title.js` (the title and the end card; not part of the kit, but they are the kit's showpiece)
Scenes `title` (6.8 s) and `end` (7.5 s) register on `window.__EP19_SCENES`; `window.FP19A` exports `TEASER20` (the one constant that names the next episode), `bevelText`, `star`, `meas`, `vgrad`, `glowAt`, `tileWall`, `counter`, `gingham`, `CREAM`, `INK`, `GOLD` for a later card. The title is the slow cooker (`F19.dish('slowcooker')`, `steam`, `light`, `hop`, `lid`, `hero`) with `F19.steam` bursts on the cues; the end card is a corkboard with the sign-up sheet, the cast chips, the stamp, the teaser and a `turkeyMold` mascot. Their cue times are read from `python3 /tmp/shift_tools/cues.py 19 title` / `end` (title: lidrattle 1.2 and 3.2, steam 1.8, textding 5.4; end: typing 1.1, stamp 4.0, textding 5.1) and snapped to frames.

## Traps
- Draw a dish AFTER what it stands behind and BEFORE what stands in front of it; the kit never reads the scene. Order dishes on a table by their `y`.
- A sprite is baked at the density the current transform needs (rounded up to a ladder of rungs), so a slow zoom is free and a fast zoom bakes a new rung or two once. Memory is capped (about 160 MB, then the cache is dropped and rebuilt: pixel-identical).
- Do not draw the same text twice at one place (a sticker in a hand AND the sticker on the plate). Do not draw a prop in flight and the same prop in a hand in the same frame.
- Steam is live: pass the scene time `t`, and a different `seed` for every dish, or two cups steam in lockstep.
- `pour` returns `{ lands, ends }` from a call with `t: -9`; do not use the first real call's result before `t0` (it is the same, but it draws nothing).
- The nominal cost: a dish 0.3 to 1.6 ms warm, 6 to 18 ms the first time (the bake); the slow cooker about 2.5 ms (stew, bubbles, steam), a pour about 1.3 ms, a blender about 1.6 ms, a sticker about 0.3 ms. Measured with `node f19_test.js cost` and `cold`.

## Testing (not loaded by index.html)
`f19_demo.html` renders every case on its own (`?case=coffee&t=1.2&bg=wall`). `node f19_test.js sheet <case,...> <t,...> out.png [cellW] [bg]` writes a contact sheet to `build/ep19_food/` (backgrounds: neutral, wall, dark); `node f19_test.js list` names the cases; `node f19_test.js det` proves determinism (every case twice, forward and in reverse time order, and in a second fresh page = a second worker, identical pixels); `node f19_test.js cost` and `cold` time the cases warm and cold; `node f19_test.js engine <case,...> <t,...> out.png` draws cases through the real renderer (grade, bloom, UI kit text).
