## Episode 14: The Survey

A nature documentary about the State. A deadpan narrator watches the colony (the staff of Fruit Bowl Rehab & Nursing) in its habitat, the building, shown as a cutaway dollhouse. At 3:02 PM a white sedan pulls into the lot: Mr. Stateman, the State surveyor, the Starfruit from Episode 2 ("Don't mind me. I'm just going to observe."). The alarm call, a text message, crosses the building in eleven seconds; the staff stand up like meerkats; the colony adapts (the shower room "now holds the second floor", every insulin pen is born today, three weeks of charting in nine minutes, call lights answered in nine seconds, the Administrator's display of pizza). The entrance conference: everything is in the binder ("Is there anything that isn't in the binder?" "The staff."), and the surveyor and his trainee Kumquat pick one nurse to follow, through binoculars: Strawberry, per diem, not on the sheet. The tracer: she runs ahead of him putting every call light within reach; he finds the one insulin pen she missed ("It's day one." "The label says June." "It's a long day."); Mr. Banana slips for the forty-ninth time ("That is a fall." "That is a hobby."); the shower room door is opened and the stash lands on everyone ("Is the Christmas tree a tag?" "It is a tag."). Alone with the surveyor, the residents speak ("The girl with the badge sits with me. She says it's nineteen sixty-two. So it is." ... "Give her a break."). In the exit conference a Plinko board picks the severity (F-725, sufficient nursing staff, lands on L: immediate jeopardy), and then comes one new tag, F-999, "The facility failed to cover its staff." ("That is not a real tag." "It is now."). The plan of correction: thirty minutes, seated, mandatory. Strawberry sits; a call light rings; the surveyor: "Don't mind me. I'm just going to help." The end card is a specimen wall and teases Episode 15, The Merger.

## Episode 13: The Float Pool

Hour four of eight, and Strawberry's break has said "pending" since the merger. "That's not a break. That's a heist." The float pool, the people no schedule can find, plans to steal thirty minutes: a hologram blueprint of the facility (thirty-two rooms, two nurses, one called out), a freeze-frame crew intro (the Brains, the Ghost, the Talker, the Muscle, the Distraction, the Eye in the Sky), and three obstacles: the call lights, which become a red laser grid that cannot see a per diem badge (a slow-motion walk through the beams, "BADGE: PER DIEM / NO MATCH"); the Administrator, who loops every fourteen minutes and can be held for forty with slides; and the Q word. The break room is locked by policy 14.3 (staff may not enter during a shift), and Strawberry is per diem, so she is not staff. Behind the door, found by penlight, is Brenda, a prune in scrubs who went on break in 2019: "Nobody covered." Strawberry says "I've got it. Go." Brenda: "Nobody ever said that." Strawberry sits and takes one bite; the Moon, on lookout, says "It's quiet out here"; every call light in the building goes off; and the crew takes over: "We've got it. Sit." The last word is "Pending." The end card teases Episode 14, The Survey.

## Episode 12: The In-Service

A mandatory in-service that is voluntary for per diem staff. The Administrator's sign-in sheet ("Persimmon is not attending. Agency."), four slides (Dignity First; answer every call light within two minutes; teamwork, "We are a family." "Are families paid overtime?" "Families are unpaid."; the budget, ninety-seven percent pizza and ninety-seven percent dignity), a quiz (a resident calls while you are on your break: "You are never on break."; a supervisor asks you to stay late: yes, yes, or let me check my contract) and the word that has no slide: "No." "There is no slide for no." A fire drill whose muster point is the parking lot ("Residents first. Staff second." "And administration?" "Third." "Pizza last."), where the Moon counts everyone and Persimmon is "on another budget". The Staff Engagement Awards: Most Reports Filed ("Nobody reads them." "I read every one. The good cuff is behind the Q-word sign."), Newest Hire, and Strawberry ("Per diem. Not required to attend. Attended." "Under protest." "We need you Friday." "No.") ending on "Then it's not in my contract."

## Episode 11: Welcome Back

Strawberry returns to Fruit Bowl as per diem and gets the red carpet Persimmon got: a banner (WELCOME BACK STRAWBERY, one R short again), a gift basket, a parking pass, lunch and a pen ("That's my pen." "It was a gift." "To share." "Is there a raise?" "You're per diem. You're in the other budget." "I know. I'm in it."). Clementine, the new staff nurse in Strawberry's old badge, gets twenty-eight residents to Strawberry's twelve ("It's contract math."), a lesson in saying "Let me check my contract" without one, and a sticker that reads NURSE (CONTRACT: NONE). A split screen runs the shift, Clementine sprinting through twenty-eight call lights on top and Strawberry in the spa chair on the bottom with a new GUILT thermometer next to the pay meter, until the thermometer shatters and she bolts. The invoice is a nine-hour minimum of helping at two hundred dollars an hour; the Administrator opens his portrait, spins the vault wheel and floods the office with gold ("Oh. We do have that one." "And the staff budget?" "That's this."), and a moth flies out of a purse. At 10:59 PM overtime would cost a hundred and seventy-seven dollars an hour, so for once everyone wants her out the door; at 11:00:00 she leaves, tells the Moon the night was "Quiet.", and the building goes up behind her ("She said the word!" "Not my shift."). The end card teases The In-Service.

## Episode 10: Two Weeks' Notice

Episode 9 ended on a SHIFTGO job post at a hundred and eighteen dollars an hour. At 2:58 PM Strawberry walks into the Administrator's office with a letter ("I'm giving you my two weeks' notice." "Is this about the pen?") and the budget finally gets a face: a wall chart that is 97 percent pizza ("You had the budget for pizza." "That's the pizza budget."). ## Episode 6: Alarm Fatigue (earlier in this PR)

Thirty-one alarms are going off and four of them matter. - The text gate (`txaudit.js`, new): the box audit never looked at text drawn into the world, so upside-down sheets, names printed over each other and posters sliced by the frame edge got through. The gate hooks `fillText` on every canvas and records each string with its true on-screen quad (camera, rotation and mirroring included). For an episode with `textGate: true`, `node render.js audit` fails mirrored, upside-down, tilted (more than 12 degrees), skewed, cut-off, overlapping, covered and container-breaking text; a second render of each frame with every string painted in a flat key colour measures how much of each string's ink really reaches the picture. Text the viewer must read is held to 30 px, a 40 px safe margin, the caption zone and the clock pill; set dressing produces informational `NOTE` lines that do not fail the audit. `TEXTGATE=1` holds an older episode to the same rules and `render.js textinv` lists every distinct string with where it is most visible.
